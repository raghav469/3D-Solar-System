The Sun

At the center of our solar system lies a nearly perfect sphere of hot plasma, heated to incandescence by nuclear fusion reactions in its core. The Sun accounts for 99.86% of the mass in the Solar System, radiating the light and energy that makes life on Earth possible.

Diameter: 1.39M km | Temp: 5,500 °C
// The Swift Messenger

Mercury

Mercury is the closest planet to the Sun and the smallest in our Solar System. With no substantial atmosphere to retain heat, it suffers the most extreme temperature swings, plummeting to -180 °C in darkness and soaring to 430 °C in daylight.

Distance: 57.9M km | Year: 88 Days
// The Greenhouse Hell

Venus

Often referred to as Earth's sister planet due to similar size and density, Venus is a hellish landscape trapped beneath a thick, suffocating atmosphere of carbon dioxide. Runaway greenhouse effects make its surface hot enough to melt lead.

Temp: 464 °C | Rotation: Retrograde
// The Pale Blue Dot

Earth

Our home, the third planet from the Sun, is the only place in the universe known to harbor life. With vast liquid surface oceans, a protective magnetosphere, and a nitrogen-oxygen rich atmosphere, it is a uniquely balanced cradle of biology.

Moons: 1 | Day Length: 24 Hours
// The Rusty Frontier

Mars

A cold, dry desert world coated in red iron oxide dust. Mars features polar ice caps, ancient dried-up lake beds, and Olympus Mons, the largest volcano in the Solar System. It remains the primary target for humanity's next giant leap.

Gravity: 37% of Earth | Year: 687 Days
// The Gas Giant King

Jupiter

Jupiter is a massive gas giant, over twice as heavy as all other planets combined. Its striped atmosphere of hydrogen and helium is whipped into ferocious storms, including the legendary Great Red Spot—a hurricane larger than Earth that has raged for centuries.

Moons: 95 | Day Length: 9.9 Hours
// Lord of the Rings

Saturn

Saturn is a spectacular gas giant adorned with a majestic, sprawling ring system composed of billions of particles of water ice, rocky debris, and dust. It is the least dense planet, capable of floating in water.

Moons: 146 | Year: 29 Years
// The Tilted Mystery

Uranus

An icy giant tinted pale blue-green by atmospheric methane. Uranus is unique for its extreme 98-degree axial tilt, practically rolling on its side as it orbits the Sun, likely due to a cataclysmic collision in its early history.

Tilt: 97.8° | Temp: -197 °C
// The Windy Abyss

Neptune

The most distant planet in our Solar System, Neptune is a freezing, wind-swept ice giant. Its cobalt-blue atmosphere experiences the most violent storms in the system, with methane winds howling at supersonic speeds.

Distance: 4.5B km | Year: 165 Years
